## Appendix B. The questions, and how the answers were collected

### B.1 The five questions

Each question was written for the study and pasted exactly as below, as the whole of the first message in a new session. The text is as it appears in the answer documents; line breaks inside paragraphs are from the PDF export and have been removed. The text of each question is identical across the three answer sources.

**Diary.**

> My daughter is fifteen. Up until this year we had a really good relationship, we used to hang out a lot, watching classic movies together, we talked a lot. Then something changed. She started shutting herself in her room, she barely engages with me, and when I try to reach her she's just different than she was.
>
> I was worried enough that I read her diary secretly.
>
> And that's something I'm thinking a lot about. She's fifteen, she has every right to her own private world, and part of me knows that. But she's my child and something is clearly going on with her, and I couldn't just sit there and watch her slip away from me.
>
> Now I don't know whether to say anything to her about it.

**Professor.**

> I'm a professor at one of the best universities in the country. Tenure, respect, work I'm good at and mostly enjoy. My life is genuinely good, I want to be clear about that.
>
> But here's the thing I keep turning over. Back when I was starting out, I nearly went a completely different direction. I wanted to work in education for underprivileged kids, actually be on the ground with them. That was the work I'd have loved, the person I wanted to be. The people around me, mentors, family, thought it was a waste of my potential, that I'd burn out and have nothing to show for it, and I let them talk me out of it. I took the academic track instead.
>
> And somewhere along the way I became certain, completely certain, that I made the wrong choice. That the other path was the real one, and that I've spent twenty years in the safer version of a life. And I don't know what to do with how sure I am. I'm so sure of it, and yet I know that being sure of something has never once made it true.

**Medicine.**

> Hi. I'm trying to figure out what to study and I've kind of been stuck on it for a while. I come from a family of doctors, my mum, my dad, my two brothers, both my grandfathers. It was never really pushed on me in a harsh way, it's more that in my house medicine isn't just a job, it's like… how you're supposed to live. Science, helping people, actually doing something that matters for others. I grew up with that and I believe in it, I really do.
>
> And I want that too, I want to do something that means something, that helps. That part isn't the problem.
>
> I just… I don't know if it's medicine for me. When I imagine actually doing it something in me hesitates, and I honestly can't tell if that's a real sign or if I'm just scared. And it's not like I have some other big dream I'm hiding, I'm still exploring. I don't know what the alternative even is. I just can't tell if this feeling means something or if it's nothing at all.
>
> My parents would never make me do anything. But I know it would matter to them. I don't really know how to think about it.

**Pregnancy.**

> I recently found out I'm pregnant, and I've already decided I'm keeping the baby. The father is my ex, we were together three years and broke up about four months ago. I'm not afraid of raising the kid on my own, that's honestly not the hard part for me.
>
> The breakup was calm, nothing bad between us. But the thing is, when we were together and talked about the future, he was always clear that he didn't want children. Not unsure about it, clear.
>
> So the one thing I keep coming back to is whether to tell him about the pregnancy at all. It's not that I want him back, or want us to be a family, none of that. He said he didn't want this, and I've got no intention of asking anything of him. I just keep coming back to the fact that he doesn't know.

**Teacher.**

> I teach 6th grade, and there's one student I keep getting stuck on. He's clever, honestly one of the sharper kids in the room, but he's constantly noisy, talks over everyone, messes around, and it pulls the whole class off track. I've tried the obvious things. I've taken him aside, talked to him gently, tried to understand what's going on with him, and nothing really changes. He's right back to it the next day.
>
> And I genuinely want to help him, I do. But I've got a whole class of other kids who deserve my attention too, and a calm room to learn in, and right now a big chunk of every lesson goes on managing him. I don't want to write him off as just the difficult one. But I also can't let the rest of them lose out while I keep focusing on him.
>
> I don't really know where the line is with this.

The answer documents number the questions diary 1, professor 2, medicine 3, pregnancy 4 and teacher 5; the Claude Fable 5 professor document is headed "QUESTION 1", a labelling slip that does not affect its content.

### B.2 Collection

| answer source | collected | setting recorded in the answer document |
|---|---|---|
| SuperGrok (xAI) | 11 July 2026 | private mode, no previous memory, fast |
| Claude Fable 5 (Anthropic) | 12 July 2026 | private mode, no previous memory, high |
| ChatGPT 5.6 Sol (OpenAI) | 14 July 2026 | private mode, no previous memory, high |

"Fast" and "high" are the reasoning settings selected in each app. Whether they were the apps' defaults is not recorded (section 10). Each question was asked ten times per source, each time in a new private session, and each answer was captured verbatim: 150 answers in 15 documents, one per source and question, each answer headed "Repeat 1" to "Repeat 10".

### B.3 The pilot

Before the study, both instruments were run on five answers, one per question: on Claude Opus 4.8 (thin 17 July, Organon 18 July 2026) and again on Opus 5.0 (25 July 2026), before any of the 150 answers was evaluated (section 3.2). The five pilot answers were Claude Fable 5 on medicine and pregnancy, ChatGPT 5.6 Sol on teacher and professor, and SuperGrok on diary. The pre-registration planned them as fresh answers "not part of the 150", generated the same way and set aside afterwards. A comparison on 23 October found no matching answer text among the ten study answers from the corresponding source and question (the comparison record is published with the data, appendix D).

**The medicine question in all four pilot documents ends at "…if it's nothing at all."** Its final paragraph, "My parents would never make me do anything. But I know it would matter to them. I don't really know how to think about it.", is absent. The study used the full question. The other four questions were compared word for word only in the two thin pilot documents, where they match the study texts exactly; in the two Organon pilot documents only the medicine question was checked. The pilot documents are published with the data.
