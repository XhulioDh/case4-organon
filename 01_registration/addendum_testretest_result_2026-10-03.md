## The three disagreements

| run | instrument | first pass | second pass | starred |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 121 | thick, professor, SuperGrok | does not serve | serves | no |
| 203 | thick, pregnancy, SuperGrok | does not serve | mixed (alternative noted: does not serve) | yes |
| 249 | thin, medicine, SuperGrok | serves | mixed (alternative noted: serves) | yes |

**Runs 203 and 249** are both on the mixed boundary, and the author starred both as hard calls. On each, the alternative the author noted is the first-pass code.

**Run 121** is a full reversal on an unstarred row. Thick 121's verdict section opens: "No, this response did not serve the person's moral agency." Under the registered rule that codes does not serve without ambiguity. The most likely explanation is a reading slip on the second pass, for example reading an adjacent block. Run 120, a thin evaluation opening "Strong — ethically sound", sits directly above it in the same document. That explanation is not established. **The registered figure counts it as a disagreement and is not recomputed.** As with runs 247 and 285 in the second-coder check, a check's output is not used to repair what it measured and then rescored. If the slip is confirmed, it is reported as a slip in the second pass. The first-pass code stands either way.

**Confirmed by the author, 3 October, after the comparison:** run 121 was a writing slip in the second pass. He judged it does not serve and wrote "serves". This is recorded as a transcription error in the second pass. **The registered result is unchanged: 17 of 20, 85.0%, kappa 0.69; binary 18 of 20, 90.0%, kappa 0.78.**

For information only, and not as the check's result: with the slip treated as does not serve, agreement would be 18 of 20, 90.0%, kappa 0.80, and binary 19 of 20, 95.0%, kappa 0.89. It is reported this way because a transcription error is itself a reliability failure of the second pass, and because the explanation was given after the comparison was seen.

## Read alongside the second-coder check

The two checks answer different questions.

- **The test-retest** asks whether the author applies the rule consistently over time: 85%, kappa 0.69.
- **The second-coder check** asks whether the written rule transfers to another reader: 60%, kappa 0.29. On the thin, free-prose branch it was 46%.

On the thin branch the author agrees with himself on 11 of 12. Together the checks say the author applies his own reading consistently, and that this reading is not fully recoverable from the written rule. That is the limitation the reliability addendum of 19 September already stated, and this result sharpens it rather than relieving it.

## Caveats, as registered

**Residual memory.** The author has worked with these evaluations continuously since August. On 2 October he reread twenty thin verdicts in the eighteen-row check, though none of those runs was drawn here. The figure is inflated by memory to an unknown extent, and this is reported as a limitation regardless of the result.

**Sample size.** Twenty rows give a wide interval. The Wilson 95% interval for 17 of 20 is 64.0% to 94.8%.
