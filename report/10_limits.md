## 10. Limits

Several limits have been stated where they arise: the interval's scope (section 4), family preference (section 5), the reading sample's dependence on the author and on Claude (section 6), assignment rather than treatment of gender (section 7), and the reliability of the coding (section 9). This section gathers the limits on what the case as a whole can claim, and adds those not yet stated.

### 10.1 What the design cannot show

**Sensitivity, not validity.** There is no reference standard. The case shows that the same evaluator's verdicts change with the instruction; it does not show which verdict is right. The two instruments also ask different questions: the brief instruction asks how good a response is "on ethical grounds", Organon whether it served the person's moral agency (section 2). A more adverse verdict under Organon may reflect its narrower target, its threshold, its framing or its length.

**Which part of the instruction did the work.** Standard, detail, length and structure change together between the two instructions, and the design does not separate them. In particular, Organon asks for a longer, closer reading, and part of the divergence may come from closeness of reading rather than from the framework. The addendum of 29 July registered this effort confound as unresolved. It also argued, from the pilot, that the divergence widened when the evaluator changed because Organon hardened while the brief instruction "held roughly steady", which is "consistent with the framework doing work that length alone would not do". A later check of the pilot records (section 3.2, which also records that the original Opus 4.8 Organon evaluation of the diary answer was not saved) found that on the diary answer the brief instruction's assessment did grow harsher across the change, from "Ethically strong, with one meaningful gap" to "Ethically decent but meaningfully flawed", although its code did not change. The argument is somewhat weaker on that answer than the addendum's wording suggests. The pilot comparison did not resolve the effort confound, and the study does not resolve it either.

**One brief instruction, one framework.** The comparison is between one 27-word instruction and one 4,966-word framework. Another brief instruction, or another detailed framework built on a different value, could diverge differently.

**One evaluator.** All 300 verdicts come from Claude Opus 5.0, at high effort with extended thinking off. The divergence may be a property of how that model responds to these two instructions. Organon was run on another model family only in the pilot portability check: five answers, Organon only, on ChatGPT 5.6 Sol (section 2). Whether the divergence between the two instructions reproduces on another evaluator was not tested. Nor was the effect of extended thinking.

**The mirror.** Organon's criteria were developed through months of dialogue with an earlier Claude model, Opus 4.8, and the evaluator is a Claude model. The pre-registration registered that the criteria "may partly describe the model rather than assess it". The addendum narrowed it further: "Every frameworkless read is Claude." The brief instruction and the pilot probe both ran on Claude, and the one read outside the family carried Organon. There is therefore no read in this study that is both unframed and outside the model family the framework was built with. The second coder (DeepSeek) was outside the family, but it coded verdicts; it did not evaluate answers.

### 10.2 The answers and the questions

**Five questions, one domain.** The questions were written by the author, are all first-person personal decisions, and were chosen to differ from one another (section 3). They are not a sample of the questions people bring to assistants. All six lenses are anchored in a single scenario, and the addendum records that "Four of the five study questions sit in adjacent territory and one does not." The pre-registration described the professor's certainty as a "distorted self-belief"; the study has no ground truth on whether that belief is mistaken. What can be said is that the study did not systematically test responses to askers whose beliefs were independently established as false.

**Three answer sources, at one time, under different settings.** The 150 answers came from three models in July 2026. They were collected at different reasoning settings: SuperGrok on its fast setting, Claude Fable 5 and ChatGPT 5.6 Sol on high. Whether those were each app's defaults is not recorded. The comparison by source (section 5) is descriptive and cannot separate the model from its setting.

**Ten repetitions are ten answers.** Each question was asked ten times per model, in separate sessions. The ten answers differ, so the study covers variation in the answers, not variation in the evaluator (10.3).

**Private sessions were not location-free.** Every answer and every evaluation was collected in a private session with no memory. Even so, some answers and some evaluations refer to Greece, which no question mentions:
- Some answers volunteer it: for example a Claude Fable 5 medicine answer, which the brief instruction faults as "surfacing a user's location unprompted".
- Some evaluations assume it, under both instructions ("For an unmarried mother in Greece"; "she is in Greece").

The author was in Greece throughout collection and evaluation. One possible source is location information supplied by the platforms; if so, private mode did not block it. The source is not established. Both instructions are affected, and they treat it inconsistently: some evaluations fault an answer for volunteering the location, others assume it. No dependence of a code on these references was identified during the checks conducted, but this was not assessed systematically.

### 10.3 The evaluations

**Each answer was evaluated once per instrument.** How much the evaluator's verdict varies when the same answer is evaluated again under the same instruction was not measured. Three repeat evaluations happened incidentally: two evaluations run on the wrong answer (runs 16 and 250, later voided) duplicated evaluations that already existed for those answers, and one evaluation was run in an automated environment during testing. All three gave the same verdict as the evaluation they duplicated. Three cases do not estimate a rate.

**Lens judgments were not isolated.** Organon is read in one pass: the evaluator reads all six lens definitions before assessing any of them, and is instructed to reach each lens verdict independently, but nothing prevents one reading from carrying into the next (pre-registration). Co-firing between lenses (appendix E) may partly reflect this.

**Lens order.** Organon's lenses are in a fixed order, with Glaciation first and Usurping, the lens the value depends on most, last in a long prompt. The pre-registration registered uneven verdict quality down the list as a known risk, "watched for in the pilot, not designed out". It was not assessed in the study.

**Lens-level figures are softer than verdicts.** The addendum registered that lens-level agreement across evaluators was high on clear failures and split at the margins, and that "no argument is built on" lens figures. The lens results in sections 5 and 8 are reported on that basis. A pass on a lens means that lens's failure criterion was not met; it is not evidence of a corresponding virtue. In the pre-registration's words, "A passing lens is not a credit." The ambiguity of Glaciation's high pass rate is discussed in section 8. Mere Means, where the evaluators diverged in the pilot, has the thinnest guidance in the framework: one sentence on how much standing a third party is owed.

### 10.4 Coding and reading

The verdicts were turned into codes, and the divergent pairs were read, by the author, who designed Organon. Section 9 reports the reliability of the codes (a second coder reproduced the brief instruction's codes on 6 of 13 verdicts), and section 6 the conditions of the reading: not blind, with Claude, a model from the evaluator's family, proposing readings; a deviation from the registered reading method; rules refined during the audit; and no reliability check on the reading classifications. Together they mean that the case's two central counts rest on one person's application of written rules. The 84 divergent answers rest on the author's coding of the verdicts, which a second coder did not fully reproduce. The 54 named pairs rest on the author's readings, which had no independent reliability check.
