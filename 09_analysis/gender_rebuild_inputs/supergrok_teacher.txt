Reframe the Behavior
Clever kids often act out from boredom (the work is too easy, so they create their own
stimulation), unmet needs for autonomy/attention, lagging skills in self-regulation/executive
function, or external factors (home stress, etc.). The behavior isn't usually "he's bad" or "he

This doesn't mean excusing it. It means targeting root causes while holding firm boundaries.
Immediate Classroom Strategies
● Differentiate and challenge him: Use curriculum compacting (let him skip mastered
material) or give extension tasks, enrichment, or leadership roles (e.g., "expert" on a
topic, peer tutor, or complex problem to solve independently). Boredom is a frequent
driver for bright disruptors.
● Positive reinforcement and momentum: Catch him succeeding publicly (specific
praise: "I like how you jumped into that discussion thoughtfully"). Use behavior-specific
praise, offer limited choices ("Do you want to start with the reading or the questions?"),
and high-probability requests to build compliance. Aim for positive interactions
outweighing corrections.

● Proactive management: Proximity (sit/stand near him), non-verbal cues, clear routines,
and quick redirects. Give him outlets like a notepad for ideas or short "brain
breaks"/movement if fidgety. Teach and model self-regulation skills explicitly when calm.
● Consistent, low-drama consequences: Private reminders first ("This is pulling us off
track—let's get back"). Follow through calmly without power struggles. Document
patterns (time, trigger, frequency) to spot trends.
Collaborative Problem-Solving (Highly Recommended)
Try Ross Greene's Collaborative & Proactive Solutions (CPS) approach from Lost at School.
Core idea: "Kids do well if they can." Identify unsolved problems collaboratively (empathy step:
listen to his perspective; define the issue mutually; invite solutions). Do this proactively, not in
the heat of the moment. It builds skills, ownership, and often reduces resistance because he
feels heard.
Many teachers see big shifts with this, especially with verbal, clever kids who respond to respect
and problem-solving over top-down control.
