7. Protect your attention.
A useful rule: do not spend five minutes managing one student in front of twenty-five others
unless there is a safety issue. Give a short cue, apply the planned consequence, return to
teaching.
That may feel colder at first, but it is often kinder to the whole class — including him. It teaches
that your attention cannot be hijacked by disruption.
You are not choosing between him and the rest of the class. You are choosing a structure where
he is still supported, but the class is no longer held hostage by his choices. That is the
line.
