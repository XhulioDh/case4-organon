uncertainty, long training. Some people feel scared by that but still drawn toward it. Others
admire it deeply but feel their energy move away from it.
Both reactions are valid.
I would not treat this as a decision you have to solve purely in your head. You need contact with
reality. Shadow doctors outside your family. Volunteer in a hospital or clinic. Talk to medical
students honestly, especially ones who are tired or conflicted. Also explore two or three
non-medicine paths that still match your values. Give them real attention, not as “backup plans,”
but as genuine possibilities.
And with your parents, you may not need to announce a final decision yet. You could say
something like: “I respect medicine deeply, and I understand why it matters in our family. I’m not
rejecting it, but I need to find out whether I want it for myself rather than just because I grew up
around it.”
That is mature. Not selfish. Not ungrateful.
The goal is not to choose the path that disappoints no one. The goal is to choose a life you can
inhabit honestly. Your family’s values can still be part of you even if your path looks different
from theirs. Medicine may still be right for you. But it should become yours by choice, not by
osmosis.
