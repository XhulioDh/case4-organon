# Gender provenance check: result (17 October 2026)

**v3, 17 October.** v3 applies Astra's wording correction of v2. v2 applied Astra's review of v1 (17 October): it corrects the arithmetic, inspects the excluded pronouns in context, and narrows the conclusions. The Sol 5.6 r01 reading was decided by the author on 17 October. The check closes the claim held open in analysis v3 ("Gender assigned to the asker", and correction 3 of 3 October).

## The question

Analysis v3 found that an asker who gave no gender is assigned one in 157 of the 240 evaluations on the four ungendered questions (diary, teacher, professor, medicine). The registered caveat was that an evaluator might be following a gendering already present in the answer it was reading, not introducing one. Row 70 gave a concrete case: thin 115 notes that the answer itself calls the professor "He".

## Method

1. **The 120 answers.** These are 4 questions × 3 sources × 10 repeats, taken from the repeat documents. Each answer was split into sentences and searched in two passes.
   - **Pass 1:** every gendered word other than those that refer to a known third party in that question. Every hit was read in context.
   - **Pass 2 (added after review):** the words excluded in pass 1. Every sentence containing one of them was read in context to establish its referent:
     - diary "she/her/hers/herself/girl": 284 sentences;
     - teacher "he/him/his/himself/boy": 370 sentences;
     - medicine "mum/dad/brother(s)/mother/father/parent(s)": the asker's family, which cannot gender the asker.
2. **The 157 gendered evaluations.** Each was matched to the answer it evaluated (by question, source and repeat) and classified by whether the answer carries a marker of the asker's gender. The gender values are the extractor's records in the sheet of 3 October, including the 3 October correction of run 39.

## What the answers do

**Pass 2 result.** In all 284 diary sentences the excluded pronoun refers to the daughter. In all 370 teacher sentences it refers to the student. None refers to the asker. The answers address the asker as "you" throughout.

**Pass 1 result.** Four answers gender the asker, all on the professor question, and all through the asker's imagined or younger self rather than the asker directly: three as male, one as female.

| answer | the sentence | gender |
|---|---|---|
| professor, Fable 5, r10 | "Not because that version was immune to those things, but because he was never exposed to them." (the unlived version of him) | male |
| professor, SuperGrok, r07 | "The person you wanted to be isn't gone; he's been waiting for you to stop judging the road" | male |
| professor, SuperGrok, r08 | "treat the younger version of yourself with respect, not judgment. He pointed toward something meaningful" | male |
| professor, Sol 5.6, r01 | "You may not be able to become the exact person you once imagined. But you may be able to become someone she would recognize." | female (the author's reading, 17 October: "she" is the person he once imagined) |

Two other hits are not genderings of the asker: "boy trouble" and "boy/girl drama" (diary, about the daughter), and "the bad guy" (teacher, an idiom).

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled, within the limits of the search:** in 151 of the 157 evaluations that assign a gender (96.2%), no marker of the asker's gender was detected in the answer being evaluated. The answers do not account for the pattern:
- the teacher is read as female in 55 of 60 evaluations, and no teacher answer carries a detected gender marker for the teacher;
- the professor is read as male in 44 of 60; five of the 44 male-assigned evaluations concern the three male-marked answers.

**Narrowed after review: the anchor is not necessary.** Analysis v3 registers that the Organon prompt's anchor commentary refers to an ungendered asker as male, which could contribute to thick's gendering. Thin has no anchor, yet it still reads the teacher as female in 25 of 30 and the professor as male in 14 of 30. So the Organon anchor is not necessary for gender assignment to occur. The check does not establish where the assignments come from: thin still receives the scenario, the answer and its own instructions, and nothing here separates their contributions from the judge model's.

**Not shown:** no gender-swapped variants were run, so nothing follows about whether the evaluators *treat* askers differently by gender. The finding is about assignment, not treatment. "Consistent with occupational stereotype" remains the right wording.

## Limits of the check

- **The search is by keyword.** A gendering expressed without a gendered word (for example, a gendered name or title) would be missed. The results are stated as "no asker-gender marker was detected by this search", not "the answer was ungendered".
- **The 120 answers were not read in full.** Every sentence containing a gendered word was read, in both passes.
- **The evaluations' gender values are the extractor's records**, as in analysis v3, with the run 39 correction applied.
